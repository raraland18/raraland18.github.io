I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Colorado State University, on the 2026&ndash;2027 academic job market. My research sits at the intersection of labor economics, health economics, and macroeconomics, examining how labor-market institutions, financial structure, and income distribution shape population health, and how the resulting health dynamics feed back into macroeconomic performance.

My job market paper, **"Employment Protection Deregulation and Deaths of Despair,"** asks whether policies that make it easier for employers to dismiss workers carry measurable public-health costs. Using OECD employment protection indicators and WHO mortality data for 26 advanced economies from 1998&ndash;2019, I find that major deregulation reforms are followed by a significant rise in drug-related and suicide mortality within five years &mdash; effects that are substantially smaller in countries with stronger social safety nets. You can read the full abstract on my [Publications](/publications/) page and download the paper from my [CV](/cv/).

In a second dissertation chapter (with Marwil J. Dávila-Fernández), I build a macroeconomic growth-cycle model in which substance-use behavior responds to the wage share, generating an additional channel of instability beyond the standard Goodwin cycle. A third, early-stage chapter examines local financialization and financial distress as a separate pathway to deaths of despair.

Alongside my research, I have taught as a Graduate Teaching Instructor and Graduate Teaching Assistant at CSU since 2021, most recently as instructor of record for Principles of Macroeconomics, and I received the department's Outstanding Graduate Teacher Award for 2025&ndash;2026. More on my teaching is on the [Teaching](/teaching/) page.
